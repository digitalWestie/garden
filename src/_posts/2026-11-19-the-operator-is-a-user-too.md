Many times I've sat across the table from someone with a vision — of what they want to achieve, or of the software they're looking to build. It's a process I've always enjoyed: hashing out what's important, and how that turns into a working product.

Not everyone arrives with details, but in almost all cases a lot of thought has gone in. How it feels to be a user. What the steps involved are. What the outcomes should be. Usually things change as I inspect priorities, offer options, and ask about edge cases.

One thing I've noticed across these engagements is how little thought goes into regular operation and maintenance, compared with all of the above. Most people could go on at length about the product. Then I ask about the operational side of things, and I begin to feel like the killjoy at a party.

"We'll need some sort of admin panel I suppose…" is generally about as far as it goes.

Before I had enough experience to anticipate this lopsided consideration, I would find myself in the odd position of temporarily acting as operator for software I had just launched.

Yes, being user-centred is good. But the person operating — the person who has to update and moderate — is a user too. The hard focus in most people's minds is the launch, and what that looks like. That target (albeit an important one) can overshadow other goals: for example, to serve as an artefact for investor or sales demonstrations. A requirement to that end can sound something like: "easily spinning up an example workspace, including assets, to communicate the benefits of the software."

That sounds like a simple addition. It isn't. "Demo mode" means a parallel set of behaviours that pervade the application — skipped form validations, accelerated flows, separate user privileges.

These days I go full killjoy. I ask questions like:

- What happens a month, or a year, after launch?
- Who is the person who will sit down and operate this? What do they need to do daily, weekly, monthly?
- Will you be demoing the software? Will you need example data?
- How will you track success?
- Will you need control over x, y, and z feature?
- What kind of product offers do you want to create for customers?
- Have you budgeted for software maintenance?
